STANDARD INVESTIGATION PROCEDURES & MITRE ATT&CK FRAMEWORK MAPPINGS

1. Brute-Force Authentication (MITRE ATT&CK T1110)
Adversaries repeatedly attempt authentication across multiple common username combinations or through password spraying techniques.
Indicators:
- High volume of EventID 4625 (Logon Failure) or sshd "Failed password" notices occurring in tight chronological sequence (< 10 seconds delta).
- Target usernames frequently focus on root, admin, deploy, service accounts, and default platform identifiers.
- A sudden pivot from repeated failed logons to an accepted session (EventID 4624 / sshd Accepted) indicates potential credential compromise or successful dictionary attack.

2. Privilege Escalation & Lateral Movement (MITRE ATT&CK T1548 / T1021)
Following initial host penetration, attackers attempt local privilege elevation via sudo, su, or Windows token impersonation.
Indicators:
- Use of sudo command to spawn interactive shells (e.g., /bin/bash, /bin/sh) by non-administrative service accounts.
- Execution of commands targeting sensitive credential databases such as /etc/shadow or SAM registry hives.
- Windows EventID 4672 denoting special privileges (e.g. SeDebugPrivilege) assigned immediately after network logon.

3. Chain-of-Custody Best Practices (NIST SP 800-86)
- Digital evidence must be preserved write-blocked at point of acquisition.
- SHA-256 cryptographic hashes must be computed immediately upon ingestion and verified periodically to detect bit-rot or tampering.
- Analysis must operate strictly on forensic working copies; raw evidence records must remain immutable.
